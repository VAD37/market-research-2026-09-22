  • Silver: before/after with baseline and an unaffected control. Correlational.

What this is

Brands want to be named when someone asks an AI assistant what to buy; the market splits three ways

Organic recommendation

Being mentioned or cited in the answer without paying the engine.

Who sells: software vendors and agencies selling tools and services to raise mentions.

Paid placement

Ads in or beside AI answers, sold by the engines themselves.

Who sells: the assistant operators (for example ChatGPT's owner OpenAI, Google).

Agentic commerce

The assistant completes the purchase for the user.

Who sells: engines running checkout inside the assistant.

The three are sized, sold and measured differently. Figures from one are never added to, or compared on an axis with, another.

Load-bearing assumption 2: it works

No published case proves a sales effect: none of ~980 screened used a controlled design

~980 published cases screened (measured-by-us, 2026-09-22)

Silver, correlational: 7 as graded, or 1 under the literal grading rule. Both stand. Two of the seven run negative or null. Every positive Silver is tier 5.

Nearest signals, and their limits

Questions a sceptic will ask

The hard questions, answered only from the evidence

"$1 billion of ChatGPT ads proves demand, surely?"
It proves one engine sells ads (company-stated, 2026-08-31). Which buyers pay what: unknown in 27 of 27 segments.
"Investors put $180M into Profound. Isn't that validation?"
It validates investor appetite (vendor-reported, 2026-09-15). Filed revenue exists for 2 of 34 vendors, neither broken out.
"AI visitors convert 42% better. Isn't that proof?"
Vendor-reported, tier 4, 2026-03, and not a controlled test. Gold cases: 0.
"Is this only for big companies?"
So far, largely. 1 of 9 SMB segments shows spend; 5 of the 7 earlier spend segments were enterprise (2026-09-22).
"Will the engines leave room for anyone else?"
Unclear. OpenAI cites "more than 50" partners; Copilot's checkout "does not take a commission"; 0 of 8 publish rate cards.
"Why not just measure the answers yourselves?"
It started and stopped at a partial first day: Claude 83 of 160 runs, Gemini 24 of 160, ChatGPT and Perplexity 0.

Caveats

Appendix A1

Glossary

  • AI: artificial intelligence. AI Mode, AI Overviews: Google's AI answer surfaces in Search.
  • ARR: annual recurring revenue. Run rate: current revenue scaled to a year, as stated by the company.
  • B2B: business-to-business.
  • CPA: cost per acquisition (of a customer).
  • CTR: click-through rate.
  • EU, UK, US: European Union, United Kingdom, United States. USD: US dollars.
  • MAU: monthly active users.
  • SMB: small and medium-sized business.
  • Engine: an AI assistant or answer surface (ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, Copilot, Claude, Google AI Mode, Amazon Rufus).
  • Segment: buyer vertical × sub-market × company size.
  • Spend / attention / none: a segment's read. Attention never stands in for spend.
  • Strict / loose reading: strict counts "none" only if every signal ran; loose counts a blocked, logged channel as checked.
  • Gold / Silver / Bronze: case grades; see slide 3.
  • Tier: provenance score written on every source; lower is stronger; see slide 3.
