The answer

Brands have started paying to appear inside AI assistants, but no published evidence shows it pays back

Situation. AI (artificial intelligence) assistants reach audiences above one billion users each.
Complication. In 2026 they began selling placement inside answers, while publishers lose clicks to those answers.
Question. Which segments pay to be seen and recommended inside AI assistants, and does anything show it works?
What is asked of you. No decision is requested. Three reading choices move the figures; they are set out near the end.
The evidence supports three statements and stops at a fourth

1. Paid inventory exists and is scaling

ChatGPT ads: "$1 billion in annualized revenue run rate" (company-stated, 2026-08-31). No engine publishes a rate card or payback.

2. Buyer spend is real but thin and early

Spend shows in 8 of 27 segment cells, strict reading (analyst-derived, 2026-09-23). No buyer discloses a price paid.

3. Tool vendors are funded and consolidating

Profound: "$180M Series D at $1.8B valuation" (vendor-reported, 2026-09-15). Disclosed product revenue is small.

4. It stops at proof

About 980 candidate cases screened; zero disclose a design that isolates cause (measured-by-us, 2026-09-22).

Caveats

Caveats: self-reported figures, survivor cases, and demand read from public traces only

  • Prices, customer counts, funding and case results are self-reported; none is audited.
  • Published cases are survivors; quiet failures leave no trace. Full-page re-grading moved 12 cases up and 0 down (measured-by-us, 2026-09).
  • A spend cell shows money moved in it, never how much. Quiet spending leaves no public trace, so none and blank are weaker evidence than spend.
  • Tiers rank provenance, not correctness: a filing still carries the filer's framing.
  • Internet sources only: no interviews, no outreach. Evidence frozen at 2026-09-23; nothing later is used.
