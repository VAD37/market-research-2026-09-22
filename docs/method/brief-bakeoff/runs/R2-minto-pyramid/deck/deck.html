Brand visibility inside AI assistants: is there demand, and does it pay?

What the published evidence shows about brands being recommended, advertised and sold inside artificial intelligence (AI) assistants: ChatGPT, Google, Microsoft Copilot, Perplexity and Claude.
Research only: evidence, not a verdict
Evidence as of 2026-09
The question

AI assistants now answer buying questions; the open question is whether brands will pay to appear, and whether it works

What is happening

AI assistants (ChatGPT, Google, Microsoft Copilot, Perplexity, Claude) answer buying questions in prose. A brand appears in those answers through three markets:

  • Organic recommendation: unpaid mention, bought as software and agency work.
  • Paid placement: ads inside the answer.
  • Agentic commerce: the assistant completes the checkout.

What changed

In 2026 the engines switched on ads and checkout inside their answers, and vendors raised money on the promise. Sellers being live is not proof that buyers pay, or that paying works.

The question this deck answers

Does real demand exist, per buyer segment, and does anything show it pays?

The answer

Sellers are live and funded, and large companies are hiring; no published evidence shows it pays

Who sells: the engines

The engines own the rails: four sell ads, none publishes a price list, one discloses a checkout fee

Ads inside answers

  • Four engines sell them: ChatGPT, Google, Copilot, Amazon Rufus (company-stated, 2026-09-23). Another count exists: 5 of 8 with a live product.
  • 0 of 8 publish a rate card (a published unit price list).
  • Only stated price: OpenAI's "$3–$5 USD per click" bid guidance (company-stated).
  • Claude refuses; Perplexity is winding ads down "by the end of 2026" (analyst-derived relay).

Checkout inside answers

  • 4 live checkout programs (company-stated, 2026-09).
  • 1 of 4 discloses a fee: Copilot, 0% (company-stated, 2026-09).
  • 6 of 6 protocol fee clauses: unknown — checked 2026-09-22.
