## The question, and what the evidence supports

AI assistants — ChatGPT, Google's Gemini and AI Mode, Microsoft Copilot, Perplexity, Claude — answer buying questions in prose. A brand can appear in those answers three ways, sold as three separate markets: **organic recommendation** (unpaid appearance, bought as tools and agency work), **paid placement** (ads inside the answer) and **agentic commerce** (the assistant executes the checkout). Between 2026-01 and 2026-09 the engines switched the supply on and one vendor raised at a billion-dollar valuation. Is anyone buying, and does it pay?

**What the evidence supports:** money is moving on the supply side and in enterprise hiring; nothing published shows it pays. One engine states a "$1 billion" annualized ad run rate (company-stated, 2026-08-31); one vendor raised "$180M Series D at $1.8B valuation" (vendor-reported, 2026-09-15); 8 of 27 buyer segments carry a spend signal, nearly all of it job postings (2026-09-23). Against that: 0 of ~980 published cases use a holdout, geo-split or switchback (2026-09-22); no sub-market has a measured size; no buyer anywhere discloses a price paid.

## 3. Who is there: engines own the rails; vendors are funded, disclosure thin

**Engines.** Four sell ads inside answers on their own pages as of 2026-09-23 — ChatGPT, Google AI Overviews and AI Mode, Copilot, Amazon Rufus (a 2026-09-22 count reads 5 of 8 engines live); Claude refuses; Perplexity is winding down. 0 of 8 publish a rate card; the only price stated is OpenAI's "$3–$5 USD per click" bid guidance (company-stated, 3). Google's existing campaigns are auto-eligible — "you can't opt out". "Over 80%" of AI ad spend flows next to AI content, not inside chatbots (analyst-derived, 2026-06). Checkout: 4 live programs — ChatGPT, Google, Copilot, Perplexity; fee disclosed 1 of 4 (Copilot, "does not take a commission"); 6 of 6 protocol fee clauses unknown — checked 2026-09-22.

**Vendors.** 34 rostered on the organic side — 13 pure-plays, 16 incumbent bundles, 5 agencies (2026-09). Price disclosed 14 of 34, "$20–$999/month self-serve"; customer count 8 of 34; funding 8 of 34; filed revenue 2 of 34, neither broken out to this product. Rounds beside Profound: AirOps "$40M Series B" (2025-11), Brandlight "$30M Series A" (2026-02), Peec AI "$21M Series A" (2025-11), all company-stated, tier 5. Incumbent product revenue: Semrush "AI products surpassed $38 million in ARR", 2025-12 (filed); its add-on delta "+$60/mo" (vendor-reported). Consolidation: Semrush into Adobe 2026-04-28 (filed); Scrunch into Sitecore 2026-06-03, value "not disclosed" (company-stated) against "$225 Million" (press, tier 5); Yext–GoShine 2026-09-01 (filed). 0 of 34 vendors disclose the prompt set behind their visibility score.

## Glossary

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| AEO / GEO / LLMO | Answer engine optimisation / generative engine optimisation / large-language-model optimisation — aliases for the organic sub-market. GEO here never means geographic |
| AI Overviews (AIO); AI Mode | Google's AI answer block on a results page; Google's conversational search tab. Both distinct from the Gemini app |
| Agentic commerce | Checkout executed by the assistant on the merchant's behalf; also "transaction rails" |
| ARR | Annual recurring revenue — subscription revenue annualised |
| B2B SaaS | Business-to-business software sold as a subscription service — one of three tracked verticals |
| CMA | UK Competition and Markets Authority |
| CPA; high-CPA regulated | Cost per acquisition; the vertical where acquisition is costly and regulated — cards, insurance, supplements |
| CPC; CTR | Cost per click; click-through rate (clicks ÷ impressions) |
| Direct Offers | Google AI Mode ad pilot, unit labelled "Sponsored deal" |
| DSA; VLOSE; Art. 39 | EU Digital Services Act; Very Large Online Search Engine designation under it; its ad-repository duty |
| EU AI Act Art. 50 | Transparency obligations — AI-interaction notice, synthetic-content marking; names no assistant |

| MAU / WAU | Monthly / weekly active users, as each engine defines them; never comparable across engines |
