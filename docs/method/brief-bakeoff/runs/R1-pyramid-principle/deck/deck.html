Brand visibility inside AI assistants: demand shows in job postings and ad inventory, not yet in payback

What the published evidence says about who buys, how big the market is, who sells, and whether it works

Research only: evidence, not a verdictEvidence as of 2026-09

AI assistants covered: ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, Copilot, Claude. Acronyms are defined where they first appear and in the glossary (appendix).

Context

AI assistants now answer buying questions, and three things are sold around the answer

Artificial intelligence (AI) assistants — ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, Copilot, Claude — increasingly give the answer a shopper used to find by searching and clicking. The question this research asks: does real demand exist, segment by segment, for brand visibility and product recommendation inside those answers, and what shows it works?

Organic recommendation

The brand is named in the answer, unpaid. Sold as tooling and services to raise and measure mentions (also called GEO, generative engine optimisation).

Buyer: brand marketing, search owner

Paid placement

Ads shown inside or beside the AI answer, sold by the engine.

Buyer: media buyer

Agentic commerce

The assistant itself completes the purchase (checkout inside the assistant).

Buyer: e-commerce, payments

The three are kept separate throughout: their sizes, prices and evidence are never added together.

The answer the evidence supports

As of 2026-09-23 the evidence shows demand at the level of job postings and ad inventory, and none at the level of payback

1 · No sub-market has a measured size

Only floors from disclosed revenue, and forecasts that disagree widely.

2 · Many sellers, little disclosure

Vendors and engines rarely publish prices, revenue or method.

3 · Buyers spend in 8 of 27 segments

Mostly shown by job postings; no buyer discloses a price paid.

4 · No causal case; filed evidence runs negative

No published case isolates a sales effect.

Appendix A1

Glossary

  • AI — artificial intelligence. AI Act Art. 50 — EU transparency obligations for AI systems.
  • EU / UK / US — European Union / United Kingdom / United States. USD — US dollars. FY — fiscal year.
  • ARR — annual recurring revenue. Run rate — current revenue annualised, not booked.
  • B2B SaaS — business-to-business software as a service. High-CPA regulated — credit cards, insurance, supplements; CPA: cost per acquisition. SMB — small and medium business.
  • CMA — UK Competition and Markets Authority. Very Large Online Search Engine — EU Digital Services Act designation, carrying an ad-repository duty from 2027-01 for ChatGPT.
  • CTR — click-through rate. GEO — generative engine optimisation: content tactics aimed at being cited by AI answers.
  • Segment — sub-market × vertical × buyer size, read spend, attention, none — checked, or blank (not checked).
  • Strict / loose — strict: a still-blocked channel leaves the segment unchecked; loose: a blocked-and-logged channel counts as checked.
  • Gold / Silver / Bronze — case grades: causal / before-after with control / visibility change only. As graded / literal rule — as assigned / capped at Bronze when a required item is missing.
  • Experimental / observational reading — whether a before/after with a control counts as experimental or observational.
