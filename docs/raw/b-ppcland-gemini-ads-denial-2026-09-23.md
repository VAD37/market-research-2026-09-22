## Verbatim

Google representatives informed advertising clients during recent calls that the company plans to bring advertisements to its Gemini AI chatbot in 2026, but senior executives swiftly and publicly denied those claims on December 8, 2025. The conflicting signals highlight internal tensions around monetizing conversational AI experiences while the company already tests ads within AI Mode, its separate AI-powered search product launched in March 2025.
According to Adweek's December 8 report citing anonymous agency buyers, Google held conversations with at least two advertising clients where representatives indicated Gemini would receive ad placements targeted for 2026 rollout. One buyer familiar with the discussions stated that specific details about formats, pricing, and testing protocols remained unclear during these conversations. Both sources confirmed Google did not share prototypes or technical specifications explaining how advertisements would appear within the chatbot interface.

The advertising discussions represented what sources described as the first direct communication from Google about monetizing Gemini. One buyer emphasized the distinction between Gemini ads and existing advertisements in AI Mode, Google's AI-powered search experience that has already begun displaying promotional content to users in testing phases.
Within hours of publication, Dan Taylor, Google's Vice President of Global Ads, responded directly on X with an unambiguous statement. "This story is based on uninformed, anonymous sources who are making inaccurate claims," Taylor wrote at 8:40 PM on December 8. "There are no ads in the Gemini app and there are no current plans to change that."
The phrasing drew immediate scrutiny from marketing professionals and technology analysts who noted Taylor's use of "current plans" rather than a categorical denial of future monetization. Multiple responses to Taylor's post questioned whether the timeframe distinction mattered more than the substance. Matthew Keys, a media analyst, replied that ads appear inevitable regardless of current planning status, noting there exists "no other way to monetize Gemini."

The Gemini product line has achieved substantial scale since its February 2024 launch when Google rebranded its Bard chatbot. According to Wall Street Journal reporting, Gemini monthly active users climbed from 450 million in July 2025 to 650 million in October 2025, driven partly by the August release of the Nano Banana image generation tool.

AI Mode differs fundamentally from the standalone Gemini chatbot app. The search feature utilizes a custom version of Gemini 2.0 designed for integration with Google Search, employing advanced reasoning capabilities to address complex, multi-part questions. AI Mode reached over 75 million daily active users following global rollout across 40 languages, according to CEO Sundar Pichai's remarks during Google's third-quarter 2025 earnings call.

According to Google's specifications released during the event, this placement allows advertisers to "place your business into responses that are growing user satisfaction and engagement as consumers find information in faster and easier ways."
Adthena, a London-based AI search intelligence platform, became the first company to detect ads appearing inside AI Overview search results on November 24, 2025. The platform monitored 25,000 search engine results pages and detected just 13 instances of ads being served within AI Overviews, representing a frequency of 0.052 percent. That extremely low percentage suggests Google remains in early experimental phases despite having tested the capability for over one year since initially launching ads within AI Overviews for mobile U.S. users in October 2024.
