## Verbatim

Key takeaways (as printed): "Search isn't dying, it's being absorbed. We asked 5 industry leaders where search ends up by 2028, then tested their predictions against 328,650 marketing job postings. The market's verdict: 1 in 5 SEO job ads now asks for AI search skills (up from 1 in 8 in December 2025), those roles pay a 20.5% salary premium ($117,500 vs $97,500 median), and 28% of new AI search job titles are leadership hires. Employers are voting for evolution, not extinction."

"20.3% of SEO job ads now mention AI search, GEO or AEO skills (June 2026), up from 12.2% in December 2025. Source: SalaryGuide.com job posting data." "SEO roles asking for AI search skills pay a median of $117,500 vs $97,500 without, a 20.5% premium (US postings with advertised salaries, n=13,779)." "720 job postings in the last 12 months carry AI search, GEO or AEO in the job title itself. Almost all first appeared after December 2025." "28% of those new-title roles are leadership positions (Director, Head of, VP, Principal, Lead) vs 15% for standard SEO titles. Companies are hiring leaders, not juniors." "Demand for SEO itself is flat, not falling: SEO appears in 11-12% of all marketing job postings every month, unchanged across the period."

"As a share of ALL marketing postings: Mentions of GEO grew from 0.60% to 1.44% between December and June. AI search from 0.53% to 1.10%. AEO from 0.55% to 1.12%. Plain 'SEO' held flat at 11-12% throughout."

"Among US SEO roles with advertised annual salaries, postings that mention AI search skills carry a median salary of $117,500 against $97,500 for those that don't. A $20,000 premium that holds at both quartiles ($85k vs $72.5k at the 25th percentile, $151.4k vs $135k at the 75th)."

"720 postings in the last 12 months carry the new discipline in the title: 'SEO/GEO Marketing Strategy Lead', 'Director of SEO & AI Search', 'Principal Growth Manager, SEO/AEO', 'Digital Marketing Manager, Generative Engine Optimization'. TurboTax and Capital One are among the companies posting them. And 28% are leadership roles, nearly double the 15% rate for standard SEO titles."

Author's own commentary: "In the last 12 months, we've been briefed on titles we've never hired for before. Head of SEO and AI Visibility. GEO B2B Manager. AEO Director. Head of Experimentation. And that's everywhere from a 50-person agency to a 1,000-plus-person enterprise. The salaries reflect it too: for the most in-demand roles, we've seen offers up to 30% more than what the same seat paid before."

### The five practitioners quoted (full name, title, company)

**Andrew Holland, Director of SEO at JBH:** "Search dies within 2 years and I'll explain why. Keywords and SERPs will be viewed in a few years like cave paintings. Just go and watch any sci-fi film. Nobody searches, they ask AI... In that world you optimize for problem-solution fit, not rankings. That's brand marketing, not SEO." Author's rebuttal cites the same job-ad data: "If search were dying in 2 years, companies wouldn't still be putting SEO in 11-12% of marketing job ads every month, and 96% of the new AI search titles wouldn't still have SEO in the role." On upskilling: "The harsh reality is this is too much for many to handle. I see a lot in SEO being ousted. Sad but true. For those wanting to survive: learn brand marketing, learn content marketing, learn distribution, learn PR, or go so deep into AI you become insanely valuable." On budget: "You have to double or triple your PR budget. Budget feeds activity. Activity generates coverage. Coverage converts into brand visibility."

**JP Garbaccio, Head of SEO and AEO at Searchable:** "The query stops being text... Assistants start doing the task for you too, though that bit's more hype than reality right now (Walmart found people buying inside ChatGPT converted way worse than sending them to their own site). So near term it settles on: discover in the AI, buy on your own site... Longer term, search stops handing you ten links and starts building the answer for you on the fly. Google calls it generative UI... They tested it and people picked the generated version over the actual top website around 90% of the time." On upskilling: "Mindset flip. Stop obsessing over ranking a page, start thinking about whether you're the thing the model reaches for when it builds the answer. One question fans out into 20 sub-queries, so it's less 'am I number one' and more 'how much of the answer am I in'." On what companies should do: "Measure it, honestly. Most people have no clue if they're even getting cited in AI answers, and you can't grow what you can't see. Cheap to start now, expensive in 18 months once everyone's caught on. For agencies: AEO isn't cheap SEO, it's worth more, price it that way."

**Jules Davies, Founder at Scalerrs:** "I personally think search is simply going to be a mixture of AI chat combined with links pointing you to YouTube videos, Reddit threads, and SEO landing pages which help the user confirm what they found." On upskilling: "Search marketers need to be more intertwined with other departments. One that comes to mind is product marketing, as how the company is positioned in terms of USPs is largely going to dictate what type of content is put out there, and also what external people say about the company, which is then scraped by AI."

**Travis Tallent, Founder and CEO at Day Nova (ex MD of SEO at Brainlabs):** "Informational queries have already been absorbed by the platforms. Next, the transaction layer disappears into the platform... What gets swallowed is the middle of the funnel: comparison shopping, aggregator content, and anything that existed to help you decide. Focus on the brand before the search, structuring information for easy retrieval, and the service after the purchase." On upskilling: "Learning AI isn't a differentiator anymore; it's the table stakes to get into our industry. For those already in the industry, the real skill is knowing what to do with the time AI frees up."

**Erika Varangouli, Head of SEO and AI Search at Riverside:** "I see us moving from personalised search to contextualised search... Over time I expect contextual and agentic search to completely overtake the SERP layer, with the latter becoming mostly the backend mechanism feeding the user experience layer... Webmasters are already experiencing severe traffic loss; I only see this trend growing." On upskilling: "Brand marketing over SEO. Retrievability and semantics over rankings and strict optimisation... Half the AEO advice out there is unverifiable. The skill is running controlled tests and killing tactics that don't move anything, not adopting every new acronym."

Author's synthesis: "The consensus across all 5: pure execution skills are being absorbed by AI, so the premium moves to brand thinking, cross-channel breadth, and judgment. The hiring data agrees, roles blending SEO with AI search skills pay 20.5% more." "Our own vetting process scores candidates 60% on behavioural fit (value articulation, communication, leadership and other soft skills) and 40% on tech and domain, which is where AI fluency sits."

Methodology (as printed): "Job posting data comes from Salary Guide's job board database: 328,650 marketing-classified postings from 632,840 total postings collected between July 2025 and June 2026. Postings scaled up significantly from December 2025, so trends are reported as shares of monthly postings, not raw counts; July-November 2025 figures are directional only. 'GEO' was matched as the all-caps acronym in a search marketing context to exclude geo-targeting false positives (spot-checked manually). Salary analysis covers US postings with advertised annual salaries (13,779 postings, roughly 4 in 10 SEO postings list salary), using the midpoint of the advertised range and excluding outliers outside $20k-$500k."

FAQ answers (as printed): "Is SEO dying in 2026? Hiring data says no. SEO appears in 11-12% of all marketing job postings every month, flat across the last year. What's changing is the job itself: 1 in 5 SEO job ads now also asks for AI search, GEO or AEO skills." "How much more do AI search skills pay?
