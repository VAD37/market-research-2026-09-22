### Team structure — one person/team or two (on-site vs. off-site)

> "There's two different strategies, and it's off-site and on-site. Could be the same person or group it could be two different ones, but the strategy the skills to do both are fairly different." — Ethan Smith

[On-site work described as traditional SEO (page creation, technical SEO); off-site described as earning citations/mentions across platforms including Reddit, YouTube, LinkedIn, Instagram.]

### Hiring — in-house vs. agency, and what to screen for

> "If you have an agency who's already good at these things. Great, but if you don't, I would expect that an in-house person would be more resourceful, more adaptive." — Ethan Smith

> "There's two things. There's analysis and experiment design, and then there's dealing with ambiguity... Most people in SEO, AEO are not great at experiment design and analysis." — Ethan Smith

> "If you show up at a job without knowing how to do experiment design and analysis, you could do [learn it on the job]. But I don't know if I've ever seen that happen." — Ethan Smith

[Second hiring criterion: ability to solve ambiguous, non-prescriptive problems — Smith's example is developing a Reddit strategy from scratch, contrasted with following an established playbook.]

### Internal team composition at Graphite (agency-side, for content operations)

> "We have thirteen editors. We have multiple rounds of editing for every single piece of content that we create... we accept five percent of all applicants." — Ethan Smith

### Tools and data sources for AI-visibility tracking

> "There's the API, there's logged out scraping, and then there's logged in scraping, and then there's paid logged in scraping... you wanna consider where's the information coming from." — Ethan Smith

Tools named (per source's summary, not a direct quote): Ahrefs, AirOps, PKI [sic — possibly a transcription artifact], Surfer SEO, and EverTune for visibility tracking.

### KPI scale — prompt-set size by company stage

> "Probably one to five thousand" prompts tracked for enterprise companies; "ten to fifteen" for early-stage startups. — Ethan Smith

[Elsewhere summarized (same figure) as: "We work with later stage companies, and they'll typically do one to five thousand" prompts tracked simultaneously.]

### Measurement / attribution caveat

> "The majority of the variability of the answers is... that the answers are probabilistic... you ask the same question multiple times... seven to ten times, you'll get a decent distribution." — Ethan Smith

### Impact numbers (from the companion Graphite recap of the same interview)

> "LLM traffic converts 6x better than Google search traffic" (attributed in Graphite's own recap to a client, Webflow's, experience)
> "The long tail of AEO is 4x bigger than SEO" — Ethan Smith
> Reddit strategy: "Five good comments can transform your visibility" — Ethan Smith
> Reddit playbook: "Make one real account, say who you are and where you work, give genuinely helpful answers" — Ethan Smith
