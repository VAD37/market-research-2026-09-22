Figures, in article order:

- Sensor Tower, "newly released US data": ChatGPT mobile-app users "saw roughly twice as many ads per hour this July as they did in April" (2026).
- Advertiser count: "from around 300 in April to more than 820 this July, with at least 160 of that tally joining this month" (Sensor Tower estimate).
- April top advertisers named: Speechify, Jotform, Natural Intelligence. July top 10 named: Booking Holdings, Intuit, Home Depot, L.L.Bean. Scheels "the only company in the top 10 advertisers on ChatGPT in both April and July".
- Retail: share of ads decreased, but "retail is still ChatGPT's biggest sector by ad spend" (Sensor Tower).
- Financial services share of ad spend on ChatGPT: "rising from 2% to 12% since April"; "third largest sector after shopping and software"; BestMoney.com "one of July's top advertisers".
- Sensor Tower quote: > "ChatGPT is effectively diversifying its advertiser base, appealing to a wider swath of businesses, which bodes well for ramping monetization and expanding ad formats"
- Other July top spenders named: Expedia, GetYourGuide Deutschland, Talent Inc., Quince.
- Method line: Sensor Tower's estimates "are based largely on data from a user panel and third-party data sources" — Abe Yousef, senior insights analyst, Sensor Tower. "OpenAI declined to comment for this story."
- Ads served to free users and "$8-a-month Go plan" users.
- Sensor Tower estimate: ChatGPT app "hit 1 billion monthly users in May" (2026).
- OpenAI "reported a $100 million tally for annual recurring ad revenue in May"; comparison: Google $81.6 billion and Meta $59.3 billion ad revenue "for their most recent fiscal quarters".
- Dave Dugan (OpenAI ads chief), to BI in June: early ads placed "very conservatively"; quote: > "We've learned so much about user satisfaction, and as we've now, four months in, we've gotten more confidence about how we can deliver volume"
- Placement: ads "beneath the AI's answer to a user's prompt", not "next to queries about personal health or politics".
- Self-serve ad product "launched in May"; advertisers called it "rudimentary compared to the services run by Google and Meta".
