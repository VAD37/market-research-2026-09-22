## Verbatim

OpenAI has surpassed $100m (£76m) in annualised ad revenue in just six weeks from its ongoing ChatGPT Ads trial.
The revenue has been generated from less than 20% of eligible US-based Free and Go tier users seeing ads daily, according to a company spokesperson.
As almost 85% of Free and Go tier users are eligible to see ads, the spokesperson suggested the current revenue represents only a small fraction of ChatGPT’s broader advertising potential.
Self-service advertising on the platform is set to launch in April, and will open access to the broader advertising market.
OpenAI has more than 600 advertisers on the platform.
A geographical expansion of the pilot is underway and former Meta vice-president, global clients and agencies, has been hired to lead OpenAI’s global advertising solutions in the role of vice-president, head of global ads solutions. He started in the role this month.
While some experts and competitors have raised concerns about how consumers will respond to the new ad format, OpenAI claims it is seeing “no impact on consumer trust metrics, low dismissal rates of ads, and ongoing improvements in the relevance of ads as we learn from feedback”.
Fewer than 7% of ads are rated by users as “low relevance,” according to the company.
For advertisers, these early returns and plans for self-service ads in the near future represent an opportunity to jump on ChatGPT Ads just as its inventory increases. As eligible audiences expand, the platform’s revenue could also scale quickly.
It was revealed that OpenAI is set to raise $120bn (£91bn) last week in a funding round backed by a group of investors, including T. Rowe Price, D.E. Shaw Ventures, TPG, and Abu Dhabi’s MGX.
In addition to investment funding, OpenAI generates revenue from paid subscriptions, with roughly 60% coming from consumers and 40% from enterprise clients.
This story first appeared on Performance Marketing World.
