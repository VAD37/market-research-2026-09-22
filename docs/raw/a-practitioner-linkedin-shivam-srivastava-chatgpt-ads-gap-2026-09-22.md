**Title:** I ran ChatGPT ads. And there is a gap.
**Author:** Shivam S Srivastava
**Published:** Sep 10, 2026

The news of ChatGPT ads coming to India has created a surge of excitement among the marketing community. I was fortunate enough to have led a pilot campaign a couple of months ago for the US market.

Having spent quite some time thinking about how conversational AI could change the way people discover products and services, I was genuinely curious to see what advertising on ChatGPT would actually feel like from a marketer's perspective.

The first thing that struck me was the potential.

Unlike traditional search, where someone types a query and gets a list of results, ChatGPT users are having conversations. They are explaining problems, asking questions, comparing options and sometimes exploring a problem without even knowing exactly what solution they are looking for.

For a B2B marketer, that is extremely interesting. Because the richer the conversation, the richer the intent signal can potentially be. But after actually running the campaign, I found myself thinking about something else.

ChatGPT may know the intent, but the marketer doesn't necessarily get to see enough of it.

I wanted to understand what kind of conversations were leading to my ads. What were people actually asking about? Were they exploring enterprise AI adoption, workforce transformation, AI training, leadership or something completely different?

That kind of information would have been incredibly useful to refine the campaign, improve the messaging and understand what the audience was really interested in.

But I couldn't get that level of visibility. And this, for me, is where the difference between intent and intent intelligence becomes important.

Google gives marketers a lot of visibility into search behaviour. I can understand the search terms that triggered my ads and use that information to refine my targeting and messaging. LinkedIn gives me something else that is extremely valuable in B2B: professional identity. I can decide that I want to reach a particular industry, company, job function or seniority.

ChatGPT potentially offers something different. It can understand the context behind the question. But for a B2B marketer, understanding intent is only part of the equation. I also need enough information to learn from that intent and optimise towards something that ultimately matters to the business. And in B2B, that is rarely just clicks. It is leads, meaningful conversations, opportunities and eventually revenue.

This doesn't mean I think ChatGPT Ads is not relevant for B2B. Quite the opposite. I think the underlying proposition is fascinating, and as the advertising platform matures, conversational intent could become a very powerful signal for marketers.

My takeaway from this first experience is simply that the advertising infrastructure has some catching up to do with the intelligence of the platform itself.

For now, if I were allocating my primary B2B advertising budget, LinkedIn followed by Google would still be my first choices. ChatGPT Ads, at least based on my experience so far, feels more like a channel I would experiment with, learn from and keep a close eye on.

Because the potential is definitely there.

The interesting question is whether marketers will eventually be given enough visibility into that potential to actually turn it into actionable marketing intelligence.
