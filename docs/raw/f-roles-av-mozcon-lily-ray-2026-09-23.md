### KPIs used to measure AI search

> "Branded impressions, how frequently people are actually looking for and seeing our brand name" — cited as one of the evolved metrics for AI-search-era tracking.
> Additional KPIs named: "Share of voice in AI search"; direct and organic clicks to homepage; "AI search visibility by topic"; traffic, engagement, and conversions attributed to LLM referrers; visibility compared across different LLMs.
> Recommended method: use Google Analytics and build "custom segments like AI search" to isolate the channel.

### Tools named

Google Search Console (using regular expressions to filter queries), Moz, Ahrefs, Semrush (keyword research), "LLM tracking tools like Profound, Peak [sic]," GA4, Make.com (automation).

### Measured data cited in the talk

> "95% of ChatGPT users still rely on Google"
> AI search drives "less than 1% of total site traffic" (general claim)
> Amsive's own clients see "one to maybe now 2% of traffic coming from LLMs"
> Organic search typically represents "30, 40, 50% of overall traffic" by contrast
> Search-session frequency, pre- vs. post-ChatGPT-adoption cohorts: "10.5 sessions per week on Google" before, "12.6" after (a rise of 2.1 sessions/week) — cited as evidence that AI adoption has not cannibalized Google session volume for the same users
> "Only using ChatGPT" users are described as a "tiny blue sliver" of the total user base

### Framing quote (roles/discipline boundary)

> "why would you call it AEO? Isn't everything we do, answering queries?" — Lily Ray, on whether "AEO" names a genuinely new discipline or is a rebrand of existing SEO work.
> Closing framing (per recap): "Today's AI Search success still comes down to understanding people, not just platforms," argued as evidence that GEO/AEO tactics are "largely represent[ing] evolved SEO fundamentals rather than entirely new practices" — i.e., a direct claim against "new role" framing, from a VP-level practitioner.
