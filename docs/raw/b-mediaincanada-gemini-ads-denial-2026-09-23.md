## Verbatim

(Image: Unsplash)
Despite multiple reports that Google has begun briefing advertisers on plans to introduce ads into its Gemini AI chatbot, the tech giant insists no such rollout is in the works.
Google Canada sources denied the claim and directed Media in Canada to comments made on X by Dan Taylor, VP of global ads.
“This story is based on uninformed, anonymous sources who are making inaccurate claims. There are no ads in the Gemini app and there are no current plans to change that,” Taylor said on the social media platform.
Several U.S. media outlets reported Thursday that, in separate calls with at least two advertising clients, Google representatives indicated that ad placements within Gemini are being targeted for a 2026 rollout. Details on formats, pricing and testing remained unclear. The representatives also reportedly said the initiative would be separate from ads in AI Mode.
Launched in Canada in August, AI Mode is the company’s AI-powered search experience for advertisers designed to support exploratory queries and more complex tasks such as product comparisons, trip planning and step-by-step instructions.
Google has also made a number of other moves this year to expand its AI advertising ecosystem.
In May the company introduced AI Max for Search campaigns globally, including in Canada. And in November it rolled out Ads Advisor and Analytics Advisor. Powered by the latest Gemini models, these agents are intended to act as partners, learning from each advertiser’s datasets and real-time performance signals to improve Google Ads outcomes.
Across its broader ad stack, Google enhanced Performance Max with new controls, channel-level reporting and asset-level segmentation. On YouTube it added shoppable connected TV formats and launched a creator partnerships hub, also expanding Demand Gen with new image inventory on the Google Display Network.
The company also extended its commerce capabilities with the Canadian launch of Apparel Try On in October, following its U.S. debut in July. The tool allows users to virtually try on apparel items using a photo upload.
