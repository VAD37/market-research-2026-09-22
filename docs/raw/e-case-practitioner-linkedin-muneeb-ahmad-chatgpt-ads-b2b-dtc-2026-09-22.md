## Verbatim

**Title:** I Ran ChatGPT Ads for B2B and DTC. Only One Audience Actually Converts.
**Author:** Muneeb Ahmad
**Published:** Jun 20, 2026

Running ChatGPT ad campaigns across different verticals, and one pattern keeps repeating itself so consistently that I can almost predict it before I open the dashboard.

DTC campaigns take off. B2B campaigns plateau within days.

At first, I assumed it was creative, targeting, or budget pacing. It wasn't. It was something far more basic and far more interesting once you start digging into it.

The Pattern I Kept Seeing

Every time I launched a campaign targeting agency founders, CEOs, CMOs, or marketing directors, the early performance looked promising. Then, almost on cue, reach would flatten out, and CPMs would creep up far faster than they did on consumer campaigns.

When I pulled the audience data apart, the answer was obvious in hindsight, a huge chunk of that B2B audience already pays for ChatGPT Plus. They don't need convincing. They're not in a discovery phase. They're already inside the product, using it daily, and an ad promoting "try ChatGPT" or a ChatGPT powered tool just doesn't land the same way for someone who's already a paying user.

This is the part that doesn't get talked about enough in performance marketing circles. We obsess over creative testing and bid strategy, but platform user overlap, how much of your target audience already uses the product you're advertising, can quietly cap a campaign's ceiling before it even launches.

Why B2B Audiences Hit Saturation So Quickly

Think about who actually works in agencies, runs marketing teams, or sits in a CMO seat in 2026. ChatGPT Plus (and now the higher tiers) has become as standard a tool for this group as Slack or Notion. Founders use it for ideation. CMOs use it for campaign briefs. Agency owners use it to scope proposals.

That's great for OpenAI's retention numbers. It's not great for your audience pool when you're running paid advertising aimed at that exact demographic.

In practice, this means:

A meaningful percentage of impressions are served to people who are already paying customers, which inflates spend without driving new conversions.
Frequency caps get hit faster because the addressable, "convertible" audience is smaller than the platform's reported audience size suggests.
CPMs rise because you're competing for a thinner slice of people who genuinely fit the "not yet converted" profile.

If you're doing B2B marketing and your offer revolves around AI productivity, copywriting, or anything that overlaps with what ChatGPT already does well, you're often advertising to people who've already made up their minds one way or the other.

DTC Tells a Completely Different Story

Flip over to DTC campaigns, and the math changes entirely.

Consumer audiences skew much more heavily toward free plan usage. Most everyday users dabble with ChatGPT for occasional tasks, homework help, quick questions, and writing assistance without ever feeling the need to upgrade. That means your addressable audience for a ChatGPT related DTC offer is genuinely larger, fresher, and far less saturated.

I've seen this play out directly in acquisition cost. DTC campaigns targeting general consumer segments consistently pulled in lower CPAs and higher click through rates than B2B campaigns running the same creative logic, just swapped for a different audience.

The lesson isn't "DTC marketing beats B2B marketing." It's that audience behavior toward the underlying product determines how much headroom your media buying actually has.

What This Means for Audience Targeting

Before you launch any campaign tied to ChatGPT, OpenAI advertising inventory, or AI adjacent offers, it's worth asking a question most media buyers skip entirely:

How much of my target audience already uses this product, and at what tier?

That single question should shape your entire targeting strategy. If you're in B2B marketing, you might need to narrow further targeting smaller companies or earlier career marketers who haven't adopted paid tiers yet, rather than the senior decision makers who almost certainly have.

If you're in DTC, you likely have more room to scale broadly, since free plan saturation is lower and the audience is less likely to have already formed an opinion about the product.

Lessons for Media Buyers

A few things I now do differently before launching any ChatGPT related campaign:

Map the likely platform adoption rate of the target segment before setting budgets.
Treat B2B segments as smaller, more finite pools and plan creative refresh cycles accordingly, since frequency builds up fast.
Use DTC style broad targeting as a benchmark for what "healthy" CPMs and CPAs should look like, then compare B2B performance against that baseline rather than against itself.
Don't assume audience size estimates equal addressable opportunity. A platform might report millions in reach, but if half of them are already paying customers, your real opportunity is much smaller.

This isn't unique to ChatGPT, either. Any digital advertising campaign promoting a product with high organic adoption in a specific segment will run into the same ceiling. It's just more visible right now because ChatGPT adoption among marketers and founders has moved so fast.

Where This Leaves Us

Performance marketing has always rewarded people who understand audience behavior at a granular level, not just targeting settings. Platform user overlap is one of those unglamorous variables that doesn't show up in a case study headline, but it quietly decides whether your campaign scales or stalls.

If you're running paid advertising tied to AI products right now, the real work happens before the campaign goes live, understanding who's already in the building versus who's still standing outside.

Have you run ChatGPT ads or any AI product campaigns for B2B vs DTC audiences? I'd love to hear whether you saw the same saturation pattern, or something completely different.
