> ## At a Glance
>
> * Bitly had strong brand recognition and an existing presence across third-party sources. However, there was an opportunity to build a more intentional and consistent presence, particularly across Reddit and other sources influencing search, AI recommendations, and buyer research.
> * When buyers searched for link shorteners or QR code tools, the answers came from sources Bitly didn't write and couldn't control.
> * Foundation built a Reddit-first GEO program: a credibility-earned account, native content engineered to rank and be cited, and a brand-owned subreddit Bitly controls outright.
> * During the 12-month engagement, Bitly's citation share for link shorteners grew to 11.7%, nearly double the next competitor, supported by an integrated program spanning on-site content, Reddit, and YouTube.
> * 130+ live Reddit URLs working as evergreen assets, with referral sessions growing from 178 to 930 (up 422%) and engaged sessions increasing from 98 to 426 (up 335%).
>
> ## The Challenge
>
> Bitly was navigating a shift many brands are experiencing today. Instead of typing "best URL shortener" into Google and scanning ten blue links, buyers were turning to two key discovery channels for answers:
>
> 1. Community platforms and forums that include unfiltered experiences and product feedback from Bitly users.
> 2. LLMs like ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Gemini that pull from a wide range of sources using the query fan-out
>
> So, whether someone was using traditional or AI search to ask about link shorteners, QR codes, or click tracking, the response was assembled from sources Bitly didn't own: Reddit threads, YouTube videos, review aggregators, and third-party tools. They needed to expand their influence to the off-site sources that algorithms and buyers trust.
>
> As one of the most highly-cited domains by Google and LLMs, Reddit was the obvious first step. But Reddit punishes brands that show up looking promotional, so the standard B2B marketing playbook wasn't going to work.
