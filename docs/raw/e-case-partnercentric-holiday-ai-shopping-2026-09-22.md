## Verbatim

> Holiday shopping did not "change overnight." It just quietly crossed a line, and a lot of brands are still planning Q4 as if that line isn't there.
>
> PartnerCentric's latest research found that 43% of surveyed shoppers say they bought a product in the last three months because a chatbot recommended it. Not "they'd consider it." They did it. Which means Holiday 2026 will not be about whether AI matters. It will be about whether your brand shows up as the credible answer when shoppers ask.
>
> The big takeaway from PartnerCentric's research report, Recommended, Reviewed, Ready, is clear: AI has become the starting point for most shopping journeys. Here is how to navigate this shift to win the season (not just survive it).
>
> ## Insight 1: AI is now a major top-of-funnel channel.
>
> A growing share of shoppers are beginning inside AI assistants, but that is only part of the shift. Consumers now encounter AI-generated recommendations across multiple surfaces, from ChatGPT and Gemini to Google Search, AI Overviews, and commerce platforms. They may not consciously identify every interaction as "AI shopping," but these systems are increasingly shaping which products enter the consideration set.
>
> In the study, 27% of shoppers say they begin shopping on an AI chatbot, roughly on par with Google search (30%) and well ahead of social media (15%). That's a blunt signal. The "first touch" is moving, and brands that aren't showing up in AI answers are essentially opting out of the shortlist.
>
> But here's the part that matters even more than the headline number: shoppers are not blindly trusting these recommendations. They say they ignore AI suggestions that feel generic (31%) or unsourced (12%). Translation: Audit how priority holiday products appear across ChatGPT, Gemini, Google Search, and AI-generated search results.
>
> [...] Invest in building proof points through creators and reviews. Per the research, combining AI recommendations with creator proof can increase sales up to 6x.
>
> ## Insight 2: The shortlist doesn't drive the conversion. Most purchases close on marketplaces.
>
> [...] Data shows that shoppers still prefer traditional channels to finalize their purchases. Consumers primarily complete transactions on marketplaces (52% via Amazon or retailer sites) and in-store (19%), while social commerce accounts for 11%. Even the most tech-forward, high-intent shoppers tend to finish their journeys on a marketplace.
>
> However, the line between discovery and checkout is blurring quickly. As OpenAI's first e-commerce partner, Shopify allows merchants to sync stores, set budgets, and run labeled product ads directly inside ChatGPT. Shopify is also collaborating with Google and Microsoft to ensure products surface seamlessly across various AI assistants.
>
> Mobile connectivity is driving this shift, making it impossible to separate shopping into neat, isolated channels. According to the 2026 Global Digital Shopping Index, 53% of purchases involved a mobile device, while 47% of consumers used AI somewhere in their most recent purchase. Three in 10 used ChatGPT while researching that purchase, up from just 2% two years earlier.
>
> Holiday data backs up this trend. Adobe Analytics reported that 56.4% of US online holiday transactions in 2025 occurred on smartphones, while AI-driven traffic to retail sites skyrocketed by 693.4% year-over-year. [...]
>
> ## The bigger takeaway: AI and creators win together when you brief for evidence
>
> AI and creators are not competing for two different shoppers. In PartnerCentric's research, trust in creators was substantially higher among shoppers who also expressed high trust in AI recommendations. [...]
>
> ### FAQ
>
> What is AI shopping (and why does it matter for Holiday 2026)? AI shopping is when consumers use chatbots or AI assistants to discover products, compare options, and make purchase decisions. It matters because shoppers are already starting their journey in AI at meaningful rates, which affects who makes the shortlist.
>
> Does AI replace influencers and creators? No. The research suggests they're complementary. Shoppers who rely more on AI also tend to rely more on creators.
>
> Where do shoppers actually complete purchases if AI drives discovery? Mostly on marketplaces (Amazon or retailers) and in-store, even when discovery starts elsewhere.
>
> What's one fast improvement brands can make? Strengthen the proof layer: reviews, comparisons, clear specs, and consistent claims across product pages, marketplace listings, and creator messaging.
>
